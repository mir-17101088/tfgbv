She Did Everything Right

In 2021 a stranger flooded Nadia’s inbox with pornography. She reported it the way the law says to. Five years, four police offices and two arrest warrants later — warrants for her — she is still waiting.

She does what she is told

In between the messages, Nadia does everything a woman in her position is told to do.

Ask for advice. 22 Oct Her friends tell her to contact the Cyber Crime Unit.

Message Police Cyber Support for Women. 22 Oct They want her name, address, age, profession, father’s name, phone and National ID number — and a GD copy, and screenshots, “in clear Bangla”, in one message.

File a General Diary (GD). 24 Oct At Darus Salam police station.

Send PCSW everything. 24 Oct The information, the GD copy.

Ask for an update. 26 Oct No reply. 27 Oct No reply. 28 Oct She will be informed; fact-finding takes time.

Send all the screenshots again. 31 Oct “It will be reported again.”

Block him. Report him to Facebook. 2 Nov After being scolded for not having done it already.

Give PCSW the GD officer’s number. 17 Nov

A GD is a logged report. It is not a criminal case.

Block him, they said

On 2 November she turns to a friend who works in law enforcement.

He advised me to block the account, and said there are so many big crimes — murder, militancy and so on — that nobody will want to bother with some annoying messages.

তিনি আমাকে ব্লক করার পরামর্শ দেন এবং বলেন খুন, জংগিবাদ ইত্যাদি বড় বড় অপরাধ এত বেশি যে সামান্য বিরক্ত করা মেসেজ নিয়ে কেউ মাথা ঘামাতে চাইবে না।

When she pushes back, he blocks her. An hour later, PCSW scolds her for the same thing — not having blocked him. She answers:

Blocking, or reporting the account to get it shut down, is not a solution. He has targeted me. If he is stopped online, he will harm me offline. Are you waiting for that? If you were going to advise me to block him, why did you tell me to file a GD?

ব্লক করা কিংবা রিপোর্ট করে অ্যাকাউন্ট বন্ধ করা কোনো সমাধান না। সে আমাকে টার্গেট করেছে। অনলাইনে আটকালে অফলাইনে ক্ষতি করবে। আপনারা কি সেটার অপেক্ষায় আছেন? আমাকে যদি ব্লক করার পরামর্শই দেবেন, তাহলে জিডি করতে বললেন কেন?

PCSW’s reply:

Do you know that Bangladesh Police does not hold the account’s information — that Facebook has its own authority? You can’t just say whatever you like because you are upset.

আইডির তথ্য যে বাংলাদেশ পুলিশের কাছে থাকে না ফেসবুকের একটা কর্তৃপক্ষ আছে তা কি আপনার জানা আছে? আপনি ক্ষুব্ধ হয়ে যা খুশি তাই বলতে পারেন না।

What the law promises

On 29 November a police officer from Satkhira calls. The accused has been found. If she wants legal action, she should file a case at her nearest police station.

On paper, from here, the road is short. The police told her the case would fall under the Pornography Control Act 2012. Offences under it are cognizable and non-bailable (section 10). Once a case is filed, the law gives police 30 working days to investigate; 15 more with a senior officer’s approval; 30 more with the court’s. 75 working days at most (section 5).

They keep her sitting while they chat among themselves for about half an hour. Then the SI on the phone:

দায়িত্বপ্রাপ্ত এস আই আমাকে বললেন বাসায় চলে যেতে। মামলা করতে হলে নাকি আমাকে ডাকা হবে।

He apologises for sending her around so much.

On 4 December Satkhira calls again: the accused has been found. On 5 December she goes back to Darus Salam. “This case can’t be filed,” they say. She phones the SI, and the ASP in Satkhira. After a lot of back and forth, they agree.

Drafting the complaint takes three hours. That afternoon an officer comes to take her signature a second time, because the first version did not mention where she was.

Forty-five days after the first message, there is a case.

A warrant, for the complainant

On 11 March 2026 police from Darus Salam come to her home with a court summons in her name — a non-bailable warrant. According to the police, it is because she failed to appear in court. It says the officer should “seize and bring” her before the judge on 18 March.

She had never received a single notice from the court. She was not at home. Her father took the warrant. Her parents were worried.

At the police station nobody can tell her what case it is for. The SI tells her to just turn up; no papers, no people.

On 18 March she crosses the city from Kalyanpur in the middle of a fuel crisis. The court is deserted: the Eid holidays have begun. A few people chatting in a corner point her to the right courtroom. There is no one in it. The SI tells her to go home.

Seven months later, police from Gulshan keep calling one of her witnesses, who has since moved abroad. On 21 October 2026 a second warrant arrives. This time it says to arrest her. The officer at her door:

You are a woman. How would it look if we took you to the station and kept you overnight? If you promise you’ll appear in court next Sunday, we won’t arrest you.

আপনি মহিলা মানুষ। আপনাকে থানায় নিয়ে এক রাত রাখলে কেমন দেখায়? আপনি যদি কথা দেন যে আপনি আগামী রবিবার কোর্টে হাজিরা দিবেন, তাহলে আপনাকে গ্রেফতার করব না।

“I am the complainant in this case,” she tells him. It is the court’s order, he says. There is nothing they can do.

25 October 2026 His face

After a long five-year wait, for the first time, I see the face of the accused. For five years I have wanted to know where he found me, why he targeted me. I didn’t get the answer that day either.

দীর্ঘ পাঁচ বছর অপেক্ষার পর প্রথমবারের মতো আমি আমার আসামীর চেহারা দেখতে পাই সেদিন। পাঁচ বছর ধরে আমি জানতে চাচ্ছি আমাকে সে কোথায় খুঁজে পেল, কেন আমাকে টার্গেট করল। সেদিনও সেই প্রশ্নের উত্তর পাইনি।

The defence’s senior lawyer was ill. The junior he sent was afraid to cross-examine her; the judge pressed him; he said he could not. The judge asked her for a convenient date. Her witness abroad may be able to testify online, if a letter with the right evidence is submitted.

She is one of many

Nadia had a lawyer, witnesses and a public platform: she spoke about her case at a roundtable and a webinar with lawyers and members of law enforcement present. Hers is one of tens of thousands of complaints.

Complaints to Police Cyber Support for Women, 16 Nov 2020 – 31 Dec 2024: 42,642

And most women never complain at all. In a 2022 ActionAid Bangladesh online survey of 359 women aged 15 to 35, 63.5% said they had faced online violence — and more than 85% of those harassed stayed silent. Only 14.9% made a formal complaint.

Sources: PCSW report, via Prothom Alo (2025); ActionAid Bangladesh, via The Business Standard (2022).

If this happens to you

  1. Keep the evidence. Screenshot every message with the date, time and the account’s profile link. Don’t delete the chat; you can block after you have saved it.
  2. A GD is a record, not a case. For a cognizable offence, you can ask the police to record a case (FIR).
  3. If the police station refuses, you can go to a senior police officer (circle ASP or Superintendent of Police) or file a complaint before a magistrate.
  4. Police Cyber Support for Women takes complaints through its Facebook page and its hotline, 01320000888 (number published at launch in 2020), or call 999.
